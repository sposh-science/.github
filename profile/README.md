## About SPOSH

**SPOSH — Society for Promotion of Open Science Hardware** is a community that is still taking shape.

It grew from a simple belief: scientific tools should be **open, accessible, understandable and reproducible** — and people should be able to build, study, modify and improve the tools they use for science.

Our interests span **Open Science Hardware, scientific instrumentation, electronics, FOSS, research tools and hands-on experimentation**.

Right now, our main focus is simple:

> **Find like-minded people. Connect. Build together. Grow the community.**

SPOSH is a place for researchers, educators, students, engineers, developers, makers and curious minds who believe that better access to scientific tools can help create better science.

## What We Do

We keep it simple:

- **Build & share** open scientific hardware and software
- **Use & promote** open tools for science, experimentation and education
- **Document & validate** what we build
- **Connect people** interested in Open Science Hardware and FOSS

> **Documentation is part of the instrument — not an afterthought.**

## What We Believe

**Open by Design · Reproducible · Transparent · Collaborative · Accessible**

Scientific instruments should not have to be black boxes.

We want to understand how our tools work, document what we learn, test them carefully and make it possible for others to **build, reproduce, modify and improve** them.

## Where Our Journey Began

Our journey into Open Science Hardware began with **ExpEYES — Experiments for Young Engineers and Scientists**.

Working with ExpEYES introduced us to a simple but powerful idea: scientific instruments can be **open, affordable, understandable and hackable**, allowing students, educators and experimenters to look beyond the black box and understand the instrument itself.

That journey continues today with **SEELab**, extending these possibilities into more capable experimentation, measurement and data acquisition, and with **KuttyPy**, bringing open hardware into programming, electronics and physical computing.

These platforms, developed and supported by **CSpark Research** and their open-source communities, have been an important part of our experience with open tools for science.

For us, they are more than educational tools. We continue to explore how open platforms like these can grow from the classroom and maker space into **scientific instrumentation, experimentation and research**.

In many ways, the ideas behind SPOSH grew from this journey — **using open tools, understanding them, experimenting with them, and imagining what more we could build together.**

## Join the Community

SPOSH is still growing — and right now, **growing the community is one of our most important projects.**

We're looking for people interested in:

**Open Science Hardware · FOSS · Scientific Instrumentation · Electronics · Research Tools · Experimentation · Science Education**

You might contribute by building something, reproducing a project, testing hardware or software, improving documentation, validating an instrument, writing code, designing electronics, proposing an experiment — or simply bringing an interesting idea.
